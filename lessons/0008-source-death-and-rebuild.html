When the Camera Dies: EOS, Silence, Watchdog, Rebuild

A camera can end its stream two ways: politely (EOS) or by just stopping (crash). Viewers experience them very differently — one is a clean goodbye, the other is an invisible freeze that RTSP will never tell you about.

Mission tie-in: This is VideoPipeline's death and rebuild path, and it protects RtspProfileServer sessions: a source failure must NOT cascade into every viewer losing their session. The bridge keeps the appsrc alive and the watchdog tells the app the truth.

Concept: two deaths, two signatures

Graceful end (EOS)Crash / unplug
What happensProducer pipeline pushes EOSSamples simply stop. No event. No error.
What viewers seeClean stream end (EOS through the RTSP session)Frozen last frame, forever. RTSP keeps the session alive happily.
How the app learnsappsink "eos" signalNothing built-in — you must poll silence (a watchdog)
What the bridge must dogst_app_src_end_of_stream() — forward the goodbyeDeclare dead, rebuild producer, keep the media session

Both paths end the same way in your app: tear down the producer generation, build a new one, re-feed the bridge. The viewer-facing difference is everything.

The four rules of source recovery

1. Forward EOS when it happens — appsink "eos" → gst_app_src_end_of_stream(appsrc). Viewers get a clean goodbye instead of a freeze that looks like a bug.

2. Watchdog the silence, not the clock — sample arrival gap > threshold, valve open, no EOS → declare dead. Poll from the main loop; never from a streaming thread.

3. Never tear down the media on source loss. All viewers would drop and re-do tune-in. Keep the appsrc session; only the producer generation changes. Note end_of_stream is one-way: an EOS'd appsrc cannot accept new data — that's why the EOS case naturally ends the session (client hangs up), while the crash case reuses it.

4. Seed new sessions with the latest frame. The bridge keeps one newest buffer; new appsrc gets caps + that frame immediately. Measured: client 2's counter was already at 11 samples by 505 ms after connect.

Two subtleties we hit so you don't have to

Restart the encoder with a keyframe. Fresh x264enc starts with an IDR frame, so a decoding client resumes cleanly. If you ever restart mid-GOP (e.g. by unlinking/relinking a raw branch), decoders show garbage until the next IDR — that's why key-int-max matters in the architecture doc.

Give the rebuild a watchdog grace period. In the first run, the watchdog re-fired 16 ms after gen3 started — before its first sample arrived. Reset the silence clock when a generation starts, not only when samples flow (our first run showed the false positive; the fixed version doesn't).

Check yourself

1. The camera process segfaults. Your RTSP client shows what?

Measured: RTSP noticed nothing for 2.5 s and would notice nothing ever. Only a sample-arrival watchdog distinguishes "idle" from "dead".

2. When the watchdog fires, why should the media session survive?

Measured: gen3's frames continued the same client counter seamlessly. Tearing down media would force every viewer through DESCRIBE/SETUP/PLAY again — the exact churn the bridge design avoids.

3. Why is gst_app_src_end_of_stream() a one-way door for that session?

EOS is terminal for that appsrc. In the labs, the EOS case ends with the client hanging up (session dies naturally); the crash case keeps its session because no EOS was ever sent.
